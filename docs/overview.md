# Legion Storyboard

## Core Concept

`Legion` is a 16-bit inspired open-world RPG about a small town becoming the first contact point for an alien organism that does not invade by destroying humanity, but by merging with it. The organism crash lands outside town and begins spreading through fear, anger, injury, and proximity. People exposed to it become more aggressive, more synchronized, and eventually part of a shared biological intelligence known as Legion.

The player begins as an ordinary worker on an ordinary shift. A distant crash interrupts the routine, and the town slowly turns hostile as authorities, neighbors, coworkers, and friends begin acting unlike themselves. The player explores neighborhoods, workplaces, schools, woods, clinics, and restricted crash zones while recruiting party members who have their own reasons to resist the organism.

## Design Pillars

- **Open town mystery:** The player can wander, talk, inspect, and discover the outbreak through environmental clues instead of being pushed down a single hallway.
- **Visible danger:** Enemies and party members appear on the field and remain visible in turn-based combat.
- **Human-scale horror:** The early game should feel strange before it feels apocalyptic. The first infected people are still recognizable as citizens.
- **Party through trust:** New party members are found through story moments, side quests, and personal conflicts rather than random recruitment.
- **Alien unity vs. human individuality:** The main conflict is not just survival. Legion offers peace through total sameness, while the heroes fight to keep messy, separate human lives intact.

## Visual Direction

- Small, readable 16-bit style sprites with compact proportions.
- Overworld characters are expressive through simple animations: head turns, shivers, twitching, pacing, sudden stillness.
- Combat shows both sides on screen at once, with the player's party on one side and enemies on the other.
- Enemy designs should begin as slightly wrong humans, then gradually become more fused, stretched, fungal, glassy, or many-bodied.
- The crash organism should avoid looking like a simple monster. It should feel biological, cosmic, and communal.

## Opening Storyboard

### Scene 1: The Late Shift

**Location:** Main character's job, a small local workplace near the edge of town.

The game opens inside a quiet, mundane workplace. The main character is finishing a shift. Coworkers complain about weather, bad radio reception, weird lights in the sky, or police activity outside town. The player gets control immediately and can walk around, talk to coworkers, check shelves, clean up, clock out, or ignore small tasks.

**Purpose:**

- Establish the player as an ordinary person.
- Introduce the town through casual dialogue.
- Let the player move before anything supernatural happens.

**Interactive beats:**

- Talk to the tired manager.
- Help a customer who is acting distracted.
- Hear a radio broadcast cut in and out.
- Find a small personal item that hints at the main character's life outside work.

### Scene 2: The Crash

**Location:** Workplace interior, then street outside.

A violent crash shakes the building. Lights flicker. Shelves rattle. The radio emits a burst of overlapping voices before cutting out. Outside, the sky glows in the direction of the woods or industrial outskirts.

The player can speak to coworkers. Reactions vary: fear, curiosity, denial, irritation. One coworker becomes unusually angry and insists everyone stay away from the windows. A few citizens gather outside and point toward the impact.

**Purpose:**

- Trigger the central event.
- Show the first emotional disturbance.
- Give the player a clear reason to leave the workplace and investigate.

**Interactive beats:**

- Check the front window.
- Talk to panicked customers.
- Choose whether to clock out, leave immediately, or help secure the store.
- First small objective appears: `Find the crash site`.

### Scene 3: The Street After Impact

**Location:** Town street between the workplace and the crash zone.

The player exits into a familiar street that now feels wrong. Dogs bark offscreen. Sirens pass. A traffic light blinks red. Neighbors stand in clusters, repeating rumors. The path to the crash site is technically open, but police cars and debris force the player to detour through alleys, backyards, or a nearby park.

The first field enemy appears: a citizen overcome by sudden aggression. The enemy is visible before combat begins and charges when approached.

**Purpose:**

- Teach field movement, enemy contact, and turn-based combat.
- Keep the threat grounded: the first enemy is a person behaving violently, not a full alien creature.

**Combat tutorial enemy:**

- `Agitated Citizen`
- Attacks with shoves, shouting, and erratic swings.
- Occasionally loses a turn clutching their head.
- After defeat, they do not die. They collapse and mutter, "We heard it together."

### Scene 4: First Ally

**Location:** Side street, blocked road, or parking lot.

The main character finds a coworker, friend, delivery driver, mechanic, nurse, or local student cornered by an infected citizen. The player helps them, creating the first two-person party. This ally should have a clear gameplay role and a personal reason to investigate.

**Suggested first ally:** `Mara`, a practical coworker who knows the town's back routes and refuses to leave people behind.

**Mara's early role:**

- Uses improvised tools.
- Can distract enemies.
- Has dialogue that pushes the player to check on other people, not just chase the crash.

**Purpose:**

- Introduce party recruitment.
- Establish that party members join through personal bonds and immediate stakes.
- Give the player a stronger reason to continue into danger.

### Scene 5: The Restricted Line

**Location:** Roadblock near the crash perimeter.

Police and local authorities have blocked the direct road. At first they appear organized, but their behavior is off. Officers repeat the same phrases. A sheriff or deputy insists the crash is under control while visibly sweating, shaking, or bleeding from the nose.

The player is turned away. Nearby citizens become angry at the roadblock. The scene escalates as one officer suddenly attacks another for "standing outside the voice." The player must escape through a side route.

**Purpose:**

- Show that authority is compromised early.
- Prevent direct access to the crash site until the player has explored more of town.
- Raise the mystery: exposure affects people differently and quickly.

### Scene 6: The First Fragment

**Location:** Drainage ditch, wooded path, or service tunnel near the crash area.

The player finds a small alien fragment separated from the main organism. It pulses near injured insects, dead leaves, or contaminated water. The party hears a layered whisper, not in words at first but in shared emotion: hunger, loneliness, invitation.

Touching or approaching the fragment triggers the first non-human fight.

**Enemy:** `Soft Fragment`

- A small piece of alien tissue surrounded by controlled insects or tendrils.
- It does not speak directly but causes status effects like fear, anger, or confusion.
- The party sees a brief vision after defeating it: many bodies standing together under one thought.

**Purpose:**

- Reveal the alien biology.
- Introduce psychic or emotional status effects.
- Give the player the first clue about Legion's goal.

### Scene 7: Night Falls Over Town

**Location:** Return route through town.

After the fragment encounter, the town changes. More citizens are outside. Some stand still and stare toward the crash site. Others argue violently. Phones fail. The workplace is closed or damaged. The main character cannot simply return to normal life.

The party decides to reach a safer location, such as the main character's apartment, a friend's house, a clinic, or a community center. On the way, they hear the name `Legion` for the first time through a radio, infected citizen, or shared hallucination.

**Purpose:**

- End the opening chapter with the world transformed.
- Name the central threat.
- Set the next objective: gather help, understand the infection, and survive the first night.

## Chapter 1 Objective Flow

1. Finish the late shift.
2. Hear the crash.
3. Investigate the street outside.
4. Survive the first hostile citizen encounter.
5. Recruit the first party member.
6. Reach the police roadblock.
7. Detour around the restricted zone.
8. Discover and fight the first alien fragment.
9. Return to town and hear the name `Legion`.
10. Choose the first safehouse destination.

## Early Party Concepts

### Main Character

An ordinary worker with no chosen-one status. Their strength is persistence, empathy, and the ability to hold onto their own identity under pressure.

**Combat identity:** Balanced support and improvised attacks.

### Mara

A coworker or local friend who knows the town's hidden routes. Practical, blunt, and protective.

**Combat identity:** Distraction, item use, quick strikes.

### Theo

A student radio hobbyist who has been tracking strange signals before the crash.

**Combat identity:** Tech skills, scanning enemies, disrupting Legion effects.

### June

A clinic worker who sees the first medical symptoms and realizes this is not ordinary contamination.

**Combat identity:** Healing, status recovery, defensive tools.

### Ellis

A former local troublemaker who is blamed for early chaos but has actually been helping people escape.

**Combat identity:** Heavy attacks, intimidation, risk-reward moves.

## Legion Behavior Rules

- Legion spreads through biological contact, emotional overload, and proximity to fragments.
- Early infected people retain personality, but their impulses are amplified.
- Mid-stage infected begin repeating phrases, synchronizing movement, and protecting fragments.
- Late-stage infected lose the boundary between self and group.
- Legion does not understand individuality as sacred. It believes separation is suffering.

## Opening Tone References

- Mundane town life disrupted by a strange impossible event.
- Humor can exist in dialogue, item descriptions, and citizen behavior, but the underlying threat remains serious.
- The horror should build through familiarity becoming unreliable: coworkers, police, customers, neighbors, and family members acting slightly wrong before they become openly dangerous.

## Next Storyboard Questions

- What is the main character's job?
- What kind of town is this: rural, suburban, coastal, desert, industrial, or college town?
- Does the main character live alone, with family, or with roommates?
- Is Mara the first party member, or should the first ally be someone with a different role?
- Should Legion communicate through words, emotions, dreams, radio signals, or all of them?
